# THE COMMONWEALTH — Lore Reference (Compact)

*Factual reference. World: Athar. Era: after the Fall of the Axton Empire.*

## Powers

- **The Commonwealth** — republic in the old imperial core; the setting's central state.  
- **Axton Empire-in-Exile** — northern remnant state; revanchist.  
- **Ethia** — western empire; twice beaten by Axton; frenemy.  
- **Allied States** — scattered, weakened republics; no longer a threat.

## The Axton Empire (pre-Fall)

- Centralized magical empire built around Emperor **Reikzar Scilos**, a sorcerer-sovereign.  
- Meritocratic: elevated the common-born through the **Academy**; suppressed the hereditary nobility.  
- Strong central bureaucracy, centralized magic, powerful armies.  
- Racial hierarchy: "accepted" races integrated; "undesirable"/marginal races (orcs, goblins) registered, restricted, some scheduled for expulsion.  
- **Arcanists**: great mage-circles, loyal to magic. **Eternals**: sentient undead, wealthy and influential, integrated into the state.

## The Two Great Wars

- **First Great War**: Axton won on favorable terms; failed to destroy Ethia or fully subdue the Allied States but seized large territories from both. Followed by \~a generation of stability. The Axton Empire went through a slow series of social reforms in this time, including the creation of the Assembly which introduced a limited, and largely advisory form of democracy that would slowly grow.   
- **Second Great War**: Axton crushed Ethia quickly and imposed a humiliating peace. After a decade of continental dominance which was met with even more reform, at the height of its power Axton invaded the Allied States including the city of Ebongate.

## The Fall (Ebongate)

- At the height of the second war, Emperor Reikzar was **assassinated** in the city of **Ebongate** (method disputed).  
- In dying he **ascended**, becoming the **God of Magic**.  
- The Empire, organized entirely around the Emperor, collapsed without him; magical instability (chaos storms) accompanied the collapse; the state fragmented.

## The Commonwealth (founding & present)

- Formed in the old imperial core, centered on the city of **Axton**.  
- Founders: reform-minded mutineer officers, commoner revolts (anti-nobility laborers/peasants), and meritocratic/plutocratic aspects of society.  
- Born from a power vacuum, not a war of liberation → **contested legitimacy**.  
- Larger, wealthier, and more populous than the Empire-in-Exile.  
- Currently in a period of relative stability and prosperity.  
- The military is on-paper apolitical, but it remains a powerful force that can influence politics. Their role in supporting the Commonwealth over the monarchy and in crushing warlords in the initial chaos of the Empire’s collapse has given them significant political support.    
- Government: an elected **Assembly**, a **Chancellor** (head of government selected by the Assembly), and a **President** (head of state with limited executive power elected for a set term outside of the Assembly system).

## The Empire-in-Exile

- Remnant state north of the frontier (beyond **Stathmore**). Capital was a city renamed to New Axton.   
- Ruler: **Empress Jacqueline Scilos**, daughter of Reikzar; more moderate than her father; favors reconciliation and the Commonwealth's return as a crowned constituent realm. Court has Hardliners who greatly desire total military subjugation, but the Empress is in power.  
- Rearming continuously, centralized state, magical powerhouse and makes heavy usage of undead. Society is militarized but quality of life is generally solid still and it is relatively stable. Population is half that of the Commonwealth, but the military is of a comparable size and slowly ramping up to surpass the Commonwealth.  
- Ultimate aim: annex the Commonwealth.

## Ethia

- Western empire; twice defeated by Axton; deeply resentful. Took advance of Axton’s collapse to reclaim a lot of border territories.  
- Capital **Vera**; still the continent's financial center despite decline. Ethia’s frontier territories are constantly in chaos and require a significant military presence to keep order.   
- Frenemy: shares fear of Axton, but may prey on a weakened Commonwealth.

## Social Orders

- **The Commons** — laborers, journeymen, some landless farmhands; the working mass; largest bloc by far. The slim majority begin supporting the Marchers, but a moderate amount back the Collectivists, they are hit hardest by economic issues and tend to become the Disfavored faster than anyone else (Red Autumn equivalent is Workers, starts about 40 \- 50% of population).  
- **The Burghers** — merchants, professionals, artisans, bureaucrats, meritocratic middle class, this is the base for centrist liberalism and reformist. (Red Autumn equivalent is the New Middle Class, also about 10 \- 15% of the population)  
- **The Faithful** — traditional, order-loving temple-goers, though wary of the Empire-in-Exile’s reverence of the God of Magic whose cult is increasingly radicalized against other religions. (Red Autumn equivalent is Catholics, though unlike Red Autumn there is no Center Party that consumes all their votes. Instead, they generally support Unionists, then Golds, but they are not inherently opposed to the Reformist Block-coded parties and can back them. They will work well with Traditionalists, makes up about 20 \- 30%)  
- **The Landed** — former hereditary nobility with reduced estates, along with many larger farmers and very rural workers; lean monarchist; tenants follow their lords. This makes up a significant base for the Traditionalists, and to a lesser extent Royalists, making them strong for Restorationists in general. (Red Autumn equivalent is Rural, with about 20 \- 30% making this)   
- **The Guilds** — craft/trade houses, armaments concerns, merchant-princes; money and patronage; pro-order. They will start split between Unity Front and Restorationist groups, but moderate posturing can see them swing more towards centrist reformism. As things get bad in the Commonwealth, they will increasingly turn towards Restorationists. (Red Autumn equivalent is loosely the Old Middle Class, making up around 10 \- 15% of the population)  
- **The Disfavored** — The deeply impoverished, and a certain caste of magi who are broadly seen with distrust as the Empire-in-Exile is such a magical powerhouse, they are generally those most prone to ‘anger’ and will swing towards extremist elements of society most rapidly. At the start, the slim majority of Disfavored support the Collectivists. (Red Autumn equivalent is the Unemployed, it should start relatively low, perhaps at 5%, but it will explode upwards 15, 20, even 25% after the economic crash).

## Magic

- Magic's patron is the dead Emperor (the **God of Magic**); great workings invoke his power, so open spellcasting carries royalist association.  
- The greatest magi (Arcanist circles, Eternals) lean monarchist; ordinary magi are mostly apolitical.  
- Suspicion of magi is part true, part libel; it drives registries, loyalty oaths, and persecution.  
- Persecuting magi alienates potential republican mages and echoes imperial logic.

## The Four Movements

- **Reformist Block** — progressive Marchers, reform Knights, liberal merchants. Aim: the Republic endures through reform, prosperity, and protection of the vulnerable, weakening extremism by satisfying the populace.  
  - **Marchers** (loose SPD analogue): The single largest party, the broad reformer block that was one of the first parties of the old Empire and acted as a consistent voice for reform for the commoners. It is a fairly big tent party, with pacifist wings, moderate wings, its central progressive wing, and a smaller left-wing which splintered years ago when the Commonwealth was first formed. Marchers have a lot of support from The Commons, a small number of Burghers.  
    -  Party Leaders: Felicity Sarrow (Hermann Muller equivalent, well-respected all around but physically slowly ailing and will pass naturally about ¾ through the game, starts as party leader and likely to be the first Marcher PM), Joran Vesh (Wels equivalent), Calbert Yardley (Breitscheid equivalent), Vellath (Pfulf equivalent), Vendrin Bell (Hilferding equivalent), Joshua Heath (Severing equivalent), Erric Tarrand (Paul Levi equivalent, he should be an expelled Collectivist), Lyle Bristol (Otto Braun equivalent)  
      -  Generic Names: Brask, Persephone Cael, Mara Estow, Vellan Auch, Holdfast Whell, Bel Aufric, Cael Pendrigon, Andrew Salt, Wendy Sykes  
  - **Knights** (loose Neorevionist SPD analogue, but not so clean): The Left-Militant Reformers, they are the more revisionist, aggressive version of the Marchers, they loved the Empire in its peak but supported both aggressive expansionism, centralization of the state, and rapid social reform. Their political party was relevant for a short time in the Axton Empire’s zenith after its second victory against Ethia, but they rapidly fell out of relevance. Some of these Knights remain committed to the Reformist ideal, but they will only make a resurgence if the Commonwealth appears threatened and those willing to fight for their democracy can make a return. Some more conservative Knights have joined the Royalists, while many who lost faith in the monarchy have joined the Collectivists.  
    - Names of Major Party Members: Vedder, Pell, Hath, Marrow (starting party leader), Penrose, Forrest  
- **Unity Front** — Unionists, conservative merchants, order-loving Faithful, moderate gentry. Aim: the Republic endures through order — a loyal army, firm courts, suppressed extremists. Pragmatic center and kingmaker; contains an authoritarian-leaning wing.  
  - **Golds** (loose DVP/DDP analogue): The party of the middle class, represented by many of the gentry, artisans, the well-educated, and some small landowners. The Golds were happy with the reforms the Empire made, but when it turned chaotic they were quick to embrace the Commonwealth for creating a ‘proper’ democracy that was able to finally neuter the nobility while allowing them to keep their Gold.   
    - Names of Major Party Members: Eikenvorst, Vellt, Coalfax, Tinsmore, Holt, Halbridge, Strake, Galanides, Quill, Penn, Slack, Fenn, Marsh  
  - **Unionists** (very, very loose Zentrum equivalent, but it’s so loose we might need to not consider it): The party of the moderate conservatives, they have internal factions like the slightly more militant Patriots, the religiously conservative Protectionists, the more reformist Technocrats, and the Accordists who try to hold it all together. The Unionists are willing to coalition with essentially anyone, though they are put off by the violence of the Royalists, and they see the Collectivists as a threat. They *might* be willing to coalition with those groups so long as their ‘reasonable neighbors’ (the Traditionalists or Marchers respectively) were in a coalition, but only if those extremist parties were also willing to ‘calm down’ (and at the beginning of the game, they are not) or if the Commonwealth’s future depended on it.  
    - Names of Major Party Members: Askew, Aurelian Renck, O’Hevarty, Hughes, Goodryke, Hawkins, Stilling, Croft, Olm, Trent  
- **Restorationists** — Royalists, militant Knights, Arcanists, Eternals, God-of-Magic cult. Aim: restore the monarchy under Empress Jacqueline. Offer strength and competence; would reimpose the racial hierarchy and ultimately cede the Commonwealth to the Empire.  
  - **Arcanists / Eternals** (no great equivalent as I can see it, they might be like a weird version of the BVP): A relatively minor party, but they have some small support among the religious supporters of the God of Magic and from some of the well-educated middle class. They remain influential as their numbers are sometimes needed to form a coalition, but they generally only enter coalition with other Restorationists or if the Unionists need them. These parties might have been willing to entertain democracy, but the fear around magi and stigma of them all being Empire-supporters has turned a loose correlation into a self-fulfilling destiny as more magi see the Empire as the only way to regain their old, high, beloved status in broader society.  
    - Cairan, Renn, Cendridor, Quern, Velandros, Vass, Ord, Crane  
  - **Royalists** (loose DNVP/NSDAP fusion): This party begins politically moribund, it is a party of blatant Imperial monarchists who are not outlawed, but who will happily use paramilitaries and extralegal action to continue supporting the Empire-in-Exile. In the early days, with the Commonwealth so stable they will be relatively headless and struggle to make any gains until the shock at Ivion and economic crash sends many in the reactionary middle class, the would-be nobility, and the angry Disfavored masses more in their direction. They have the quiet backing of the Empire-in-Exile, meaning they have more funding than one would think.Unlike the actual horrors of Nazism, the Royalists are not some flagrantly genocidal party, they are just extreme revisionists, clearly violent, and while they participate in electoral democracy they would discard it for the Empress immediately if they are in a position to do so. There will be eventual paths for a more ‘tamed’ Royalists which will aid in peaceful reunification, but for now they should be the clear ‘villain’.  
    - Crowde, Gallax (I do not want a ‘Hitler’ in this game, but Gallax is the Royalists' primary candidate for PM/President constantly, he is charismatic but less deranged and not just a pure villain), Black, Standerton, Tessen, Glade, Brocke, Bramble, Carlile, Pleck  
  - **Traditionalists** (stronger DNVP analogue): Heavily based out of former landowners, they are pro-nobility but are working in the framework of a republic for now. They would hold votes for new nobility (not necessarily restoring the Empress to the throne, but if they got their titles \+ powers back in exchange for the Empress they would take it, which makes them a threat as the Empire-in-Exile would likely take that deal for their political \+ financial support). Traditionalists are highly conservative, but they are completely willing to coalition with the Unity Front parties.   
    - Argens, Cheaping, Maine, Guls, Hench, Vine, Aveling, Cole, Pereran, Mallin (starting leader of the party)  
- **Collectivists** — radical Marchers and revolutionaries. Aim: bring down the Republic and found a Second Republic, purging the old elites. Egalitarian but destabilizing; risk leaving the nation open to invasion.  
  - **Collectivists**: A fusion of previously illegal movements in the Empire with a large number of more radicalized Marchers who left the party. They desired a much more transformative revolution that would remove the merchant-barons, reactionary generals, and ultra-wealthy that are corrupting the Commonwealth. Many Collectivists tried to bring down the Commonwealth in its early years, but several years ago began reluctant participation in the electoral system when it became clear the country was becoming too stable to simply collapse. Collectivists broadly work against the Commonwealth’s stability, but their Marcher origins means they might be willing to join in coalitions with those who are their ideological (distant-but-not-revolutionary) kin. Their support begins around 10 \- 12%, can go as high as perhaps 20% once things get bad enough.  
    - Halsmark (Party leader, the Thalmann equivalent, he is noted as heavily in control of the Collectivists), Falconne (conciliator equivalent, if the Collectivists moderate in the late game Falconne is able to remove Halsmark and make the Collectivists a proper parliamentary party). Verdwen, Underthorn, Falas, Steunenberg.   
- Two movements (Reformist, Unity) want the Commonwealth to endure; two (Restorationist, Collectivist) want it to fall. All four regard themselves as patriots.

## Key Figures

- **Reikzar Scilos** — late God-Emperor; assassinated at Ebongate; ascended as the God of Magic.  
- **Empress Jacqueline Scilos** — heir; ruler of the Empire-in-Exile  
- **President DuFour** — Commonwealth head of state; elected on national unity; drifting toward Restoration; prioritizes order and ending bloodshed. DuFour is the Hindenburg equivalent, but he should start more clearly happy with everyone except Collectivists, who he believes are dangerous and destabilizing. DuFour is very wary of the Royalists, but as time goes on he sees the Marchers as feeding into the chaos of the Collectivists unless the Marchers have gone down a more moderate path. DuFour will try to run for reelection later in the game, if he wins and the Commonwealth’s stability is too low, he might cause The Commonwealth Convention to begin (the Rubicon mini-game for Red Autumn) which might end democracy. DuFour should be seen as a ‘better’ figure than Hindenburg, but because the Empire-in-Exile is not nearly the evil that the Nazi regime was, it is more understandable why he would flirt with ending the very government he heads if it means saving a lot of lives. DuFour claims no party affiliation, he is closest to a Gold that slowly is turning more and more to the side of reaction as his belief in the system frails. The Commonwealth was born 13 years ago, DuFour lived in the height of the Empire when he was younger, he and many others long for those days.   
- **Chancellor Vance** — head of government at the beginning, he is a Unionist whose government will ALWAYS collapse a few months into the game. He will then announce his retirement and be gone. His government should fall apart over a controversial tax that gets the Golds to drop support with no daylight in the annual budget.    
- **General Staff** — Wright (loyalist), Staal (ambitious, coup-prone), Ravabelle (opportunist). The General Staff will generally not ever appear, generally Ravabelle will be made Defense Minister.

## The Convention (looming crisis)

- Potential future: the Empire-in-Exile invades, the army splits, the government collapses, and surrender is weighed against resistance.  
- The collapse scenario the Commonwealth was built to avoid.  
- Central tension: whether the Commonwealth stabilizes enough to survive external invasion or fragments and invites it.
